Torrents, finally at home on Omarchy.

A native client for one server or many. Your theme, your keys, your seeding history. Powered by qBittorrent.

The window

It answers five questions. What needs you, what is arriving, what landed today, what is giving back and what is resting. The rail asks them, and the rows answer in words before numbers.

Rows, table or space

Rows grouped under the five questions, "waiting · no seed" and "ready in 3 min" before any figure. The table as one sorted list. Or space: a map of where the disk goes, by folder, then torrent. Each folder, category, tag and question remembers its own.

Details tell the story

One fact and a sentence, then what happened to the torrent, newest first: what went wrong, the last move, when it finished, when it was added, and its lines in qBittorrent's log.

A bar for the selection

Pick torrents and a bar floats over the list with the menu's own actions: stop or start, recheck, move, category, tags, limits, delete. What an action came to shows in a strip just above it.

A title that says what you see

"2 need you · 6 arriving, the next in 7 min". The speeds sit beside it, each with its last minute drawn under it, and the filter takes the free room when you use it.

Keys when you ask for them

Hold alt and every control shows its key. Menus, tooltips, the palette and ? say them too, so the window stays quiet the rest of the time.

The omaseed menu

ctrl+. or the mark: the servers, turtle and burst, stop and start all, and the window's own rows, from the seed console to quit.

Seed console

See what you give back. qBittorrent counts your uploads but keeps no history of them. omaseed keeps its own, so a year of seeding is one key away.

Search

Search through your own qBittorrent. omaseed names, ships and scrapes no site. It asks the search plugins you installed on your server, and every request is qBittorrent's.

Answers as they arrive

One row per torrent, the other copies folded in. A plugin that is slow or fails is named, with the reason from qBittorrent's log. Rows you already have say so before you add them twice.

Your plugins, your choice

Search plugins are small Python files you choose and install on your qBittorrent, each asking one site. omaseed doesn't name, ship or suggest any, and never asks a site itself.

A rail of its own

Your plugins, ticked or not, each with how it answered; the type to search in; this session's recent searches, a click to ask again. Recent searches stay in memory and are never saved.

It knows your library

"in your library" for a torrent you have, "likely on disk" for the same name and size, and adding goes through the add sheet, which checks the files.

esc backs out

The field ends in "× esc": one press clears the words and the results and stops any plugin still asking. The next goes back to where you were.

Create a torrent

Make a torrent from a folder. For anyone, it seeds at once from where the files are. For a private tracker, it is saved for the upload, and your passkey is only ever shown as dots.

For anyone

Public trackers, DHT and peer exchange. It is added in seed mode where the files already are, nothing copied or moved, and the magnet goes to the clipboard.

For a private tracker

The announce comes from your library, the private flag and v1 are set, and the source tag is copied from your newest torrent there. Then it is saved for the upload, not added.

The round trip

Upload the .torrent on the tracker's site, then add the site's copy: omaseed knows these files and points it at them, so it seeds without downloading.

The passkey stays hidden

Never shown, never written to a log or a file of omaseed's. A pasted announce URL is used for that torrent only.

Hashing runs on the server

qBittorrent makes the torrent, so you can close the window while a band follows it. It needs qBittorrent's Web API 2.10.4 or later.

Moving

Move a whole folder, and move it back. Its torrents, then the files no torrent owns, onto another drive. Checked before anything is sent, and followed until the last one lands.

Checked before anything moves

Room on each drive, permission to write, files already at the destination and automatic management, before a single torrent is sent.

Smallest first, followed to the end

A band under the top says how far the move has got, with pause and resume, until the last torrent lands, even on a server whose disks omaseed can't see. A summary ends it.

Move…, for any selection

m moves what you picked, to places you moved things lately or the library's folders. Torrents from several folders go into one, or keep their places under a new one.

Move back, later

The last 20 moves, or 30 days of them, from a torrent's details, the log or the palette. A torrent goes back only if it is still where that move put it, and the button says what will happen.

Quitting asks first

Quitting while a move runs asks first, and a paused move picks up at the next start.

One after another

A move started while another runs waits its turn. One that takes a few seconds just says so in the footer.

Then a summary

A summary ends it, with "recheck" and "move back…". Recheck starts again only the torrents that were running before the move.

It tidies up after itself

The folders a move leaves empty are removed, up to the folder you moved. A folder that still holds anything, a hidden file included, stays.

The loose files come back too

Move back brings back the files no torrent owns that a folder move carried. A file changed at the new place since stays there, and the summary names it.

Turtle and burst

Slow it down, or open it up for an hour. qBittorrent keeps one set of alternative speeds. omaseed gives you two presets for it, and turns either on from the status line.

One set, two presets

Turtle is qBittorrent's own alternative set, as its schedule and other clients see it. Burst is written over it for 30 min, 1 h or until you stop it, and turtle is written back after, also when omaseed quits mid-burst.

Speeds against their caps

While one is on, the chip in the status line wears its colour and each speed reads against its cap. A turns off what is on, or the last one picked back on.

Adding torrents

It knows your library before you add. The add sheet looks at your Downloads folder, your disk and what you already seed, so nothing is downloaded twice.

Your newest downloads

The newest .torrent files in Downloads are waiting when you open it. Tick several to add them together.

Files already on disk

When another torrent already has the same files on disk, one click saves this one beside them, and it seeds without downloading.

Already in your library

A torrent you already have is caught before it's added. A public one's new trackers can be merged into it.

Room on the disk

What it needs and what's left on that drive: yellow when it leaves little, red when it won't fit.

Any server

This PC, a NAS, or all of them at once. omaseed is a window onto qBittorrent wherever it runs.

Finds the one on this PC

First run looks for a qBittorrent on this machine and signs in, or offers to install one.

Or any on the network

A NAS, a seedbox, the machine under the desk: qBittorrent 5.2 or newer with its Web UI on.

Several within reach

The others wait at the foot of the rail, with what omaseed last saw on each; right-click one for its menu. ctrl+s, the omaseed menu, the tray and the bar widget switch between them.

Add when it's back

The add sheet says which server a torrent goes to. While that one is down, "add when back" sends it as soon as it answers.

Says what went wrong

A server that stops answering gets a band in plain words, with "retry now", "edit login" or "switch server". One that is only slow "isn't answering", in yellow, with how long it has waited; a refused connection is "unreachable", in red.

Signs in your way

An API key, a username and password, or nothing for a server that lets localhost in. Secrets go to the system keyring.

Self-signed HTTPS

omaseed shows the certificate's fingerprint and trusts that one certificate once you say so.

Opens what you click

Magnet links and .torrent files open in omaseed, from the browser, the clipboard, a drag or your Downloads folder.

Omarchy

It looks like the rest of your desktop. omaseed reads your Omarchy theme and repaints the moment it changes. Pick a theme under the window above and this page does the same.

In your bar

The mark in the bar is the readout: the down arrow lights while downloading, the up arrow while seeding, the square says how the server is. Click it for the popup.

Speaks up when it matters

A finished download, a server that went away and came back, a disk filling up.

Keyboard first

Every action has a key, and ? lists them all, with a filter; hold alt to see them on the controls. The mouse works everywhere too: right-click menus, drag and drop, a tick at a row's start to pick several.

Every command by name

: runs any command by name and shows its key, so the palette teaches the keys. One that can't run says why. The last five you ran come first.

qBittorrent's log, in the window

A third face beside the library and the seed console (~), followed live: levels, a filter, the next warning, and enter goes to the line's torrent.

Every theme, live

All 22 bundled themes, the light ones too, with your font, base size, transparency and window rounding.

In the Omarchy menu

A Torrents row for the menu, and a window rule for its window. Opaque like the other apps, or translucent if you pick it.

Any Linux, too

Built for Omarchy, and runs on any Linux with a bundled theme when Omarchy isn't there.

Screen readers

Every row, control and piece of text can be found, and controls are named by what they do, not by a glyph or a key. The bar widget too, once the shell runs on Quickshell 0.3.2 or later.

Readable in every theme

Text reads at 4.5:1 against the window in every theme. A colour that falls short is shifted just enough, and the rest are kept exactly. The copy on this page does the same.

Two skins

A second look, not a theme. Console redraws the whole window in place: its own faces, kana beside the headings, cut corners and brackets on the cursor. It sits over any Omarchy theme, so the colours stay yours.

Omarchy

The default: the look of the rest of your desktop.

Console

Chakra Petch for labels, Share Tech Mono for values, meters drawn as cells.

No restart

Settings › Appearance › Skin: Omarchy, Console, or follow the desktop. The window repaints at once. The switch under the window above does the same here.

Every colour is the theme's

A skin changes form only. It picks a theme role and how strong, never a colour of its own, so Console looks right in every bundled theme, the light ones too.

Its fonts are optional

Console's faces are optional packages, not bundled. Without them it falls back to the system's.

Careful with your data

It asks before it costs you a seed. Deleting files another torrent still seeds, or the last copy anyone has, gets a question first.

Shared files

Two torrents seeding the same files: deleting one asks whether the other keeps them.

The last seed

If you are the only one still seeding it, omaseed says so, and offers to stop it instead.

Why it errored

qBittorrent's API never says why a torrent stopped. omaseed reads qBittorrent's log and says it in plain words, with the keys that fix it. p stops an errored torrent, so a retry that only fails again comes to an end; e retries.

What only the log says

When qBittorrent accepts a move or a rename and then can't do it, it says so only in its log. omaseed puts it on the torrent: what didn't happen, why, where the files are and how to fix it.

Back up a qBittorrent

Its torrents to one private file, each with its save path, category, tags, limits and file priorities. Its settings to a readable file with no password in it; importing them shows only what differs, and changes nothing until you apply.

Skipped files that still take space

qBittorrent never deletes a file you skip after it was written. omaseed says what skipped files hold, and "free the space" deletes exactly those, after a sheet that says what goes.

A big tick waits, with Undo

Ticking skipped files makes qBittorrent create them at full size at once. A tick of more than 1 GiB waits 3 s with Undo, so a misclick never reaches it.

Drives that can't hold part of a file

On ntfs3, exFAT and FAT a ticked file takes its whole size at once: the add sheet and the files tab say so, and check the room first.

Powered by qBittorrent

omaseed has no torrent engine of its own. qBittorrent does the transferring, on this machine or another, and omaseed is the window you see it through.

Install

Install omaseed.

On Arch and Omarchy: one line downloads the latest release, already built, and installs it. You need qBittorrent 5.2 or newer with its Web UI on, on this machine or another.

$ curl -fLO https://github.com/diogochaves/omaseed/releases/latest/download/omaseed-x86_64.pkg.tar.zst &&
  sudo pacman -U omaseed-x86_64.pkg.tar.zst

pacman pulls Qt and the rest of what omaseed needs from the Arch repos. omaseed isn't in a repo yet, so pacman -Syu doesn't update it: for a new release, run the same line again, and omaseed --quit so the new one starts. First run finds your qBittorrent, or offers to install one.

Build from source instead 
$ git clone https://github.com/diogochaves/omaseed.git
$ cd omaseed/packaging/arch
$ makepkg -si

makepkg -si asks for your password: pacman installs the build dependencies, then omaseed.
